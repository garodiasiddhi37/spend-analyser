    How this works

    1. Clean. Rows with no supplier or no readable amount are skipped. Amounts like "£1,250.00", "1.250,00" and "(300.00)" are read, with brackets treated as credits. Supplier names are grouped ignoring case and extra spaces, and you can merge likely duplicates in Data cleansing.
    2. Convert. Every line is converted to your report currency using the rates you enter. Lines in a currency with no rate are left out and counted under data quality. We never assume exchange rates.
    3. Rank and split. Suppliers are ranked by net spend. They are marked core until the running total reaches your threshold (80% by default, the Pareto rule used in most tail spend analysis). The supplier that crosses the line is core; everything after it is tail.
    4. Compliance. PO compliance is spend with a PO number divided by all spend. Contract coverage is spend with a contract ID divided by all spend.
    5. Duplicate invoices. An exact duplicate is the same supplier, invoice number and amount appearing more than once. A near duplicate is the same supplier and amount under a different invoice number within your chosen number of days. Both need checking by hand before any recovery.
    6. Price variance. For items bought from two or more suppliers, the gap is what you would have saved buying every unit at the lowest unit price you paid in the period.
    7. Concentration. Each category's HHI is the sum of squared supplier shares, from near 0 (many small suppliers) to 10,000 (a single supplier). A consolidation candidate is a category with five or more suppliers where no single supplier holds half the spend.
